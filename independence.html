From trading company to colonial state

The English East India Company received its charter on 31 December 1600. Its first major contacts with Mughal India followed in the early 17th century. Military victories and revenue rights made it a territorial power; after the uprising of 1857, Parliament transferred the administration to the Crown in 1858.

India became an independent Dominion on 15 August 1947. Partition accompanied the transfer, and the new boundary awards were published on 17 August.

03 / How the office changed

One title, several constitutional eras

1757–1773Company governors in Bengal; no all-India Governor-General yet.
1773–1833Governor-General of the Presidency of Fort William (Bengal).
1834–1858Governor-General of India, with Company administration.
1858–1947Viceroy and Governor-General under Crown rule (the Raj).
1947–1950Governor-General of the Dominion of India; office ended when India became a republic.
Sources & editorial notes

This is a learning aid, not a complete archival catalogue. Dates are kept at year/range level where day-level evidence is not included. Some labels—such as “rebellion”, “mutiny”, “war” and “revolution”—reflect different historical traditions; the entries describe the events without treating any label as a verdict.
